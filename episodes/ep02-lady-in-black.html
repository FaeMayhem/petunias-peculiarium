The Lady in Black

Fort Warren, Georges Island, Boston Harbor

Listen: Petunia's Peculiarium on Spotify

The story

The legend says that in the winter of 1862, the young wife of a Confederate officer held at Fort Warren rowed out from Hull disguised as a man, reached her husband, and was caught. In the struggle her pistol killed him, and she was hanged at the fort in a black robe. She has been seen on the walls ever since.

No record of her trial or hanging has been found. The earliest known written version is Edward Rowe Snow's, in 1935.

Record and legend

What the record shows

  • Fort Warren held about 2,300 prisoners during the war, starting on 31 October 1861; thirteen died there of illness. (3)
  • A sentinel near the graveyard reported a white form passing him twice, and admitted he did not challenge it for fear it was a ghost. (6)
  • In January 1862, guards reported the ghost of an old woman who had lived on the island, as told by a Gloucester Telegraph correspondent. (2)
  • Women lived and worked at the fort, including the commander's wife and daughters. (5)
  • Six prisoners escaped through the loopholes in August 1863. (7)

What the legend adds

  • The bride, her disguise, the pistol, the hanging, and the black robe. No period record of any of it has been found. (1, 2)
  • The story's first known appearance in print, in Snow's 1935 book, came with his own warning: "without the slightest guarantee that any part of it is true." (2)

Further material

  • Georges Island is part of the Boston Harbor Islands and can be visited by ferry in season.
  • "Lorena," the archive song, was written in 1856 by the Rev. H. D. L. Webster, with music by Joseph Philbrick Webster.
